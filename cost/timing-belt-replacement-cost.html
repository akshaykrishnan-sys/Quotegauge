Timing Belt Replacement Cost in 2026: What Is Fair

A timing belt replacement usually costs $500 to $1,300 in 2026, parts and labor. ConsumerAffairs reports $887 to $1,302 as typical; KBB's range starts lower, around $409 to $919. Adding the water pump while the belt is off often brings the total to about $1,200.

What a fair quote includes

  • Timing belt, and usually the tensioner and idler pulleys
  • Labor, which is most of the bill
  • Often the water pump and seals if they sit behind the belt

What pushes the price up

  • Engine layout: V6 engines and transverse mounts take longer
  • Whether the water pump and seals are included
  • OEM versus aftermarket kit
  • Dealer labor rates

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Quote for a belt on an engine that actually has a timing chain
  • Belt-only price that leaves out the tensioner (it wears out too)
  • Water pump quoted separately with its own full labor charge

Questions to ask before you approve

  • Does my engine have a belt or a chain, and what is the interval?
  • Does the price include tensioner, idlers and water pump?
  • What is the labor time from the guide?

Common questions

What happens if a timing belt breaks?

The engine stops. On many engines the valves hit the pistons, causing repairs far more expensive than the belt.

When should a timing belt be replaced?

At the interval in your owner's manual, often 60,000 to 105,000 miles or a set number of years.

Do all cars have timing belts?

No. Many newer engines use a timing chain designed to last much longer.
