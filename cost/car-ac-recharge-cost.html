Car AC Recharge Cost in 2026: R-134a vs R-1234yf

A professional car AC recharge (evacuate and recharge) costs about $150 to $300 for cars using R-134a and $250 to $550 for cars using R-1234yf in 2026. Midas store prices run about $190 to $300 and $400 to $500. Most 2015-and-newer cars use R-1234yf, which costs far more per pound.

What a fair quote includes

  • Evacuating the system and checking it holds vacuum
  • Refrigerant to the factory charge weight
  • Oil and, often, leak dye

What pushes the price up

  • Refrigerant type (check the sticker under the hood)
  • System capacity
  • Whether a leak test is included
  • Dealer versus independent shop

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Repeated recharges with no leak test (refrigerant does not get used up)
  • A compressor quoted before basic pressure and leak checks
  • Charged by the can with no weight shown

Questions to ask before you approve

  • Which refrigerant does my car use, and how much went in?
  • Did the system hold vacuum?
  • Was dye added, and when should I come back for a leak check?

Common questions

Why do I need a recharge if the system is sealed?

Usually because there is a leak. A recharge without finding it often wears off within weeks or months.

Why is R-1234yf more expensive?

The refrigerant itself costs many times more per pound, and shops need separate equipment.

Can I use a DIY can?

For R-134a, cans exist but overcharging hurts cooling. R-1234yf DIY options are limited.
