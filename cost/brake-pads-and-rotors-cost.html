Brake Pads and Rotors Cost in 2026: Fair Price Per Axle

Replacing pads and rotors on one axle usually costs $250 to $600 at an independent shop or chain in 2026. AAA puts it at $250 to $500 per axle. Dealer and luxury-car quotes can reach $900 to $1,100 per axle.

What a fair quote includes

  • New pads and two new rotors on one axle
  • Hardware, slide-pin lubrication and a road test
  • Disposal of old parts

What pushes the price up

  • Larger rotors on SUVs and trucks
  • Coated or performance rotors
  • Seized caliper bolts or calipers needing replacement
  • Dealer versus independent labor rates

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Rotors replaced with no measurement given
  • Calipers added just because pads are being done
  • Both axles quoted when only one is worn
  • A flat 'brake job' price with no parts list

Questions to ask before you approve

  • What did my rotors measure, and what is the minimum spec?
  • Can the rotors be resurfaced instead?
  • Are calipers actually sticking or leaking?

Common questions

Can rotors be resurfaced instead of replaced?

Sometimes, if they are above the minimum thickness after machining. Many modern rotors are thin enough that replacement is cheaper.

Should I replace rotors on both sides?

Yes. Rotors and pads are always done in pairs on the same axle so the car brakes evenly.

How do I know rotors are needed?

Pulsing in the pedal, deep grooves, a lip at the edge, or a measured thickness below the spec.
