Oxygen Sensor Replacement Cost in 2026: Fair Range Per Sensor

Replacing one oxygen sensor usually costs $150 to $500 in 2026, parts and labor. Jerry's customer data shows most jobs at about $159 to $247; RepairPal estimates for many common cars land between $250 and $450. Upstream (air-fuel) sensors and luxury cars cost more.

What a fair quote includes

  • The sensor for the position named in the code (bank and sensor number)
  • Labor, often under an hour
  • Clearing codes and a test drive

What pushes the price up

  • Upstream versus downstream position
  • OEM versus aftermarket sensor
  • Seized or rusted sensors
  • Number of sensors replaced

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Every oxygen sensor replaced for one code
  • A sensor replaced with no code number or bank and position stated
  • Cheap universal sensors (often cause new problems on modern cars)

Questions to ask before you approve

  • Which code, which bank and which sensor position?
  • Did you check for exhaust leaks or wiring faults?
  • Is the part OEM or a direct-fit equivalent?

Common questions

Can I drive with a bad oxygen sensor?

Usually yes for a while, but fuel economy drops and it can damage the catalytic converter over time.

Does a P0420 code mean a bad sensor?

Not always. It can be the converter, a sensor or an exhaust leak; it needs testing.

How many oxygen sensors does a car have?

Usually two to four, before and after each catalytic converter.
