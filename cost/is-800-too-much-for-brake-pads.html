Is $800 Too Much for Brake pads? (2026)

Usually yes. $800 for brake pads is above the typical 2026 US range for most versions of this job. It is high for pads only, one axle, independent shop or chain, where the usual price is $150 – $300.

When $800 can be fair

  • Ceramic or premium pads cost more than semi-metallic
  • Trucks, SUVs and performance cars use larger, pricier pads
  • Electronic parking brakes on the rear can add labor time
  • Dealer labor rates are higher than independent shops

Signs $800 is padded

  • A quote with no per-axle breakdown
  • Rotors added without a measured thickness below the minimum spec
  • A brake fluid flush added at every pad change, regardless of mileage
  • Labor over 2 hours for pads alone on a normal car

What to ask before you pay

  • Which axle, and what pad brand and type?
  • Do my rotors measure below the minimum thickness?
  • Is the hardware kit included in the price?

What is a normal price?

Replacing brake pads on one axle (front or rear) usually costs $150 to $400 at a US shop in 2026, parts and labor included. Chains often advertise $130 to $300; RepairPal's national estimate is about $335 to $394. Labor is normally 0.8 to 1.5 hours per axle.
