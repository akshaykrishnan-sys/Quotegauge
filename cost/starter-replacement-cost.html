Starter Replacement Cost in 2026: What a Fair Quote Looks Like

Replacing a starter usually costs $350 to $650 at an independent shop in 2026 and $600 to $950 at a dealer. The part is often $120 to $350 (remanufactured units are cheaper). Labor ranges from under an hour to 3 or more hours depending on how buried the starter is.

What a fair quote includes

  • Starter motor (often with a core charge)
  • Labor to remove and install
  • A test that the engine cranks and charges normally

What pushes the price up

  • Starter location: some need an intake manifold or exhaust removed
  • New versus remanufactured part
  • Four-wheel-drive trucks and V6/V8 engines
  • Dealer labor rates

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Starter replaced without checking the battery and cables first
  • Labor far above the standard time for your engine
  • A relay, ignition switch and starter all quoted together without a diagnosis

Questions to ask before you approve

  • How was the starter diagnosed (voltage drop, current draw)?
  • Is the part new or remanufactured, and what warranty comes with it?
  • What is the book labor time for my engine?

Common questions

What are signs of a bad starter?

A single click with no crank, grinding, or slow cranking with a healthy battery.

Can a weak battery mimic a bad starter?

Yes. Make sure the battery and cables tested good before paying for a starter.

Is a remanufactured starter reliable?

From a known brand with a warranty, yes; it is the usual choice out of factory warranty.
