Catalytic Converter Replacement Cost in 2026: Fair Range

Replacing a catalytic converter usually costs $1,000 to $3,000 in 2026, parts and labor. Compact cars sit at the low end; trucks, hybrids, luxury cars and California (CARB) compliant converters cost more. A P0420 code does not always mean the converter has failed.

What a fair quote includes

  • Converter and gaskets
  • Labor, usually 1.5 to 3 hours
  • Clearing codes and a drive-cycle check

What pushes the price up

  • OEM versus aftermarket
  • Whether your state requires CARB-compliant parts
  • Number of converters on the vehicle
  • Rusted bolts or damaged pipes (common after theft)

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Converter condemned from a P0420 code alone, without checking oxygen sensors, misfires or exhaust leaks
  • OEM price quoted when a legal aftermarket part is available
  • No mention of what caused the failure (misfires or oil burning will ruin a new one)

Questions to ask before you approve

  • What tests confirmed the converter, not a sensor, has failed?
  • Is the part OEM, federal or CARB, and is it legal in my state?
  • What caused it to fail, and has that been fixed?

Common questions

Is a stolen converter covered by insurance?

Usually under comprehensive coverage, minus your deductible.

Can a bad oxygen sensor cause a converter code?

Yes. A faulty downstream sensor or exhaust leak can trigger P0420. Rule those out first.

Why are converters so expensive?

They contain platinum, palladium and rhodium, and compliant parts must meet emissions rules.
