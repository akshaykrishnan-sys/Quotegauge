Radiator Replacement Cost in 2026: Car and Truck

Replacing a radiator usually costs about $300 to $1,000 in 2026 at an independent shop. CarWhere's checked prices run about $250 to $460 for a common car and $350 to $915 for a truck or van. Luxury cars and dealer jobs can exceed $1,500.

What a fair quote includes

  • New radiator
  • Coolant drain and refill
  • Pressure test and leak check

What pushes the price up

  • Vehicle size and radiator design
  • OEM versus aftermarket radiator
  • Hoses, thermostat or fan added
  • Labor access (bumper removal on some cars)

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • Radiator replaced when only a hose, clamp or cap is leaking
  • A full cooling-system flush billed on top of the refill
  • No pressure test result shown

Questions to ask before you approve

  • Where exactly is the leak, and did a pressure test confirm it?
  • Is the radiator OEM or aftermarket, and what warranty?
  • Are hoses or the thermostat actually needed?

Common questions

Can a radiator be repaired?

Small leaks at hoses, clamps or the cap can be fixed cheaply. Cracked plastic tanks usually mean replacement.

Is it safe to drive with a leaking radiator?

Not for long. Overheating can damage the engine.

How long does the job take?

Typically 1.5 to 3.5 hours depending on the vehicle.
