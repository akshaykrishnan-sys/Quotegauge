Wheel Alignment Cost in 2026: Two-Wheel vs Four-Wheel

A four-wheel alignment costs about $90 to $160 at chain shops in 2026 (Midas posts $89.99 to $159.99; Les Schwab $134.99). A front-end (two-wheel) alignment is often $50 to $100. Dealer alignments run about $120 to $200. Lifetime plans cost around $180 to $230.

What a fair quote includes

  • Measuring and adjusting camber, caster and toe
  • A before-and-after printout
  • A steering-wheel centering check

What pushes the price up

  • Two-wheel versus four-wheel
  • Vehicles with driver-assist cameras that need recalibration
  • Seized adjusters or worn suspension parts
  • Dealer versus chain

Most US shops bill labor by the hour using a published labor-time guide, so the hours on your quote should be close to the standard time for your vehicle, even if the mechanic works faster.

Red flags on a quote

  • An alignment with no before-and-after printout
  • Worn tie rods or ball joints added without being shown to you
  • An alignment sold when your car was already within spec

Questions to ask before you approve

  • Can I see the before-and-after readings?
  • Were any readings out of spec?
  • Does my car need camera recalibration?

Common questions

How often do I need an alignment?

After new tires, suspension work, or a hard pothole hit, or if the car pulls or the steering wheel is off-center.

Is a lifetime alignment worth it?

It can be if you keep the car several years and drive a lot, but read the recheck rules.

Two-wheel or four-wheel?

Most modern cars get a four-wheel alignment, even if only the front is adjustable.
